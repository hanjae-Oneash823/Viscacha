# GNINA 1.3.3 orthogonal rescoring: technical comparison with AutoDock Vina

**Analysis date:** 25 August 2026
**GNINA mode:** pose-preserving `--score_only` with the built-in default CNN ensemble
**Scope:** FYN, BACE1, and CHRNA7/CHRFAM7A validated/comparative systems
**Run ensembles:** 45
**Retained Vina poses rescored:** 738
**Maximum CPU allocation:** 16 cores

## 1. Analytical question

The Vina campaign established that canonical FYN, BACE1, and CHRNA7 could recover cognate crystallographic poses and that BACE1 deletion models and CHRFAM7A topology hypotheses produced reproducible within-system changes. GNINA was introduced to test whether those conclusions were specific to Vina's empirical ranking function.

The comparison was deliberately framed as orthogonal **rescoring**, not full redocking. GNINA evaluated the complete set of poses already sampled by Vina. Consequently, any difference identifies scoring/ranking disagreement while holding the available conformational ensemble constant. This is more interpretable than launching a second search in which sampling and scoring differences would be confounded.

## 2. Inclusion decisions

| System | Reason for inclusion |
|---|---|
| FYN canonical | Nine-run exact redocking control; tests whether GNINA preserves the Vina-validated saracatinib pose. |
| BACE1 canonical, 476, and 457 | Canonical exact redocking passed, alternate pockets passed local model QC, and all three receptors used a matched protocol. |
| CHRNA7 canonical and two CHRFAM7A faces | Canonical exact redocking passed and the two hybrids are explicit matched topology hypotheses. |

KIT, GABRA2, CACNA1D, and PDE9A were intentionally excluded. GNINA cannot rescue KIT's invalid modeled pocket, create the missing 73-aa GABRA2 receptor architecture, generate an isoform difference from identical CACNA1D coordinates, restore omitted PDE9A catalytic metals, or define an unspecified alternate sequence. Applying a CNN to those cases would add numerical output without improving structural validity.

## 4. Pose conversion

Each source Vina run contained a multi-model `docked_poses.pdbqt` and a `result.json` with pose rank, Vina affinity, and fixed-frame RMSD. Meeko reconstructed one RDKit molecule with one conformer per PDBQT model using the embedded SMILES and atom-index mapping. Every conformer was written as an independent SDF record with these properties:

- protein–drug system and analytical group;
- source Vina directory and random seed;
- original Vina pose rank;
- original Vina affinity in kcal/mol;
- fixed-frame heavy-atom RMSD to the cognate canonical ligand, where defined.

This conversion preserves chemical bond orders needed by GNINA while retaining the coordinates generated by Vina.

## 5. GNINA protocol

For each source run, GNINA received the exact receptor PDBQT recorded by Vina and the corresponding multi-record SDF:

```text
gnina \
  --receptor <source Vina receptor.pdbqt> \
  --ligand input_poses.sdf \
  --score_only \
  --cnn_scoring rescore \
  --cpu 16 \
  --device 0 \
  --out scored_poses.sdf
```

No `--minimize`, CNN refinement, flexible receptor, or new global search was requested. The built-in default CNN ensemble was used without a model override. GNINA emitted `CNNscore`, `CNNaffinity`, `CNNaffinity_variance`, `CNN_VS`, and its empirical score property for every pose.

## 6. Quality control

The scored SDF may reorder atoms and remove hydrogens, so coordinate QC compared order-independent heavy-atom signatures rather than atom indices. Each signature contained element identity plus x, y, and z rounded to 0.001 Å. Results were accepted only when the complete input and output signature sets matched.

| QC item | Result |
|---|---:|
| Expected run ensembles | 45 |
| Completed run ensembles | 45 |
| Input Vina poses | 738 |
| GNINA-scored poses | 738 |
| Run-level failures | 0 |
| Heavy-atom coordinate mismatches | 0 |

This proves that changes in selected RMSD arise from reranking existing poses, not coordinate movement by GNINA.

## 7. Statistical design

### 7.1 Primary: matched Vina-rank-1 rescoring

For each seed, the GNINA values attached to Vina pose rank 1 were retained. Alternate-minus-canonical differences were computed within seed, followed by the arithmetic mean and sample standard deviation. This is the primary affinity comparison because every group contributes exactly one pose per seed and the pose-selection rule is identical.

### 7.2 Secondary: GNINA pose reranking

For each run, the pose with maximum `CNNscore` was selected. The analysis recorded its original Vina rank, original Vina RMSD, CNNscore, and CNNaffinity. For exact canonical controls, a CNN-selected RMSD below 2 Å was considered successful pose preservation.

Within-run Spearman correlations compared `−Vina affinity` with CNNscore and CNNaffinity. Positive correlation means the methods tend to favor the same poses. No p-values were calculated because random seeds characterize search reproducibility, not independent biological observations.

## 8. Canonical-control findings

| Canonical system | Vina top RMSD | GNINA-selected RMSD | GNINA <2 Å | Vina rank selected by GNINA | Rank-1 CNNscore | Rank-1 CNNaffinity |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| FYN | 1.877 ± 0.077 Å | 2.884 ± 0.248 Å | 0/9 | 8.11 mean | 0.599 ± 0.027 | 7.570 ± 0.027 |
| BACE1 | 0.955 ± 0.006 Å | 0.955 ± 0.006 Å | 6/6 | 1.00 mean | 0.919 ± 0.007 | 7.367 ± 0.012 |
| CHRNA7 | 0.297 ± 0.012 Å | 1.847 ± 0.002 Å | 6/6 | 2.00 mean | 0.954 ± 0.0003 | 6.925 ± 0.006 |

GNINA and Vina agree completely for BACE1: GNINA selected Vina rank 1 in every run. For CHRNA7, GNINA consistently preferred Vina rank 2, but the selected pose remained below the 2 Å threshold in every run. This is a minor ranking difference within the validated binding mode.

FYN is different. Vina rank 1 passed in 9/9 runs, but GNINA consistently preferred later Vina poses and none of those CNN-selected poses remained below 2 Å. Mean within-run Vina-quality/CNNscore Spearman correlation was only 0.175. FYN should therefore be presented as a validated Vina example with an explicit cross-method ranking disagreement, not as consensus validation.

## 9. BACE1 consensus result

The primary paired results use each Vina rank-1 pose.

| Comparison | CNNaffinity, group mean | Paired alternate-minus-canonical CNNaffinity | CNNscore, group mean | Paired alternate-minus-canonical CNNscore |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Canonical BACE1 | 7.367 ± 0.012 | Reference | 0.919 ± 0.007 | Reference |
| BACE1-476 | 5.693 ± 0.019 | −1.674 ± 0.012 | 0.115 ± 0.006 | −0.804 ± 0.007 |
| BACE1-457 | 4.077 ± 0.022 | −3.290 ± 0.023 | 0.039 ± 0.001 | −0.879 ± 0.007 |

The ordering is unambiguous and agrees with Vina: canonical BACE1 is favored, BACE1-476 is intermediate, and BACE1-457 is most disrupted. GNINA-selected geometries also remained displaced: mean RMSD was 10.105 Å for BACE1-476 and 7.282 Å for BACE1-457, versus 0.955 Å for canonical BACE1. Neither alternate had a CNN-selected pose below 2 Å.

This is the strongest consensus finding in the campaign. Two distinct scoring approaches support deletion-associated disruption, and both indicate that BACE1-457 is the more severe hypothesis. The numerical CNNaffinity differences are model outputs, not kcal/mol and not measured changes in pKd.

## 10. CHRNA7/CHRFAM7A consensus result

| Comparison | CNNaffinity, group mean | Paired alternate-minus-canonical CNNaffinity | CNNscore, group mean | Paired alternate-minus-canonical CNNscore |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Canonical CHRNA7 | 6.925 ± 0.006 | Reference | 0.954 ± 0.0003 | Reference |
| Fusion at A face | 6.493 ± 0.264 | −0.433 ± 0.266 | 0.759 ± 0.109 | −0.195 ± 0.109 |
| Fusion at B face | 4.239 ± 0.297 | −2.686 ± 0.295 | 0.094 ± 0.043 | −0.859 ± 0.043 |

GNINA supports the Vina topology ordering. The A-face effect is smaller and more variable, whereas the B-face model has a much lower CNNaffinity and pose score. GNINA-selected mean RMSD was 1.847 Å for canonical CHRNA7, 3.127 Å for the A-face hybrid, and 8.857 Å for the B-face hybrid. Canonical CHRNA7 passed in 6/6 runs; neither hybrid passed.

The conclusion remains topology conditional. GNINA does not establish which hybrid pentamer is biologically assembled, so it strengthens the sensitivity result without resolving stoichiometry or orientation.

## 11. Interpretation boundaries

- CNNscore is a pose-confidence/ranking output, not affinity.
- CNNaffinity is a learned prediction and is not on the Vina kcal/mol scale.
- Vina energy and CNNaffinity must not be subtracted or averaged together.
- Rescoring the same Vina poses is scoring consensus, not independent conformational sampling.
- Repeated GNINA values inherit stochastic variation from the Vina pose ensembles; score-only evaluation itself is deterministic for a fixed receptor–pose pair.
- GNINA training-set and target-class biases remain possible, especially for modeled proteins and membrane receptors.
- Agreement between Vina and GNINA increases confidence in a structural hypothesis but does not constitute biochemical or pharmacological validation.

## 12. Presentation conclusions

The most defensible presentation statements are:

1. **BACE1 is the strongest consensus result.** Both Vina and GNINA support disruption of verubecestat recognition, with a larger effect for BACE1-457.
2. **The CHRFAM7A B-face hypothesis is consistently disruptive.** The A-face hypothesis is weaker and more variable, demonstrating topology dependence.
3. **Canonical BACE1 and CHRNA7 remain pose-valid after GNINA reranking.**
4. **FYN is a transparent method-disagreement example.** Vina passes exact redocking, but GNINA does not preserve the sub-2 Å top ranking.
